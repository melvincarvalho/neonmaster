# The prompt

Eighth game in the harsh-critic-loop series. After levels, gods, and
duels became theorems, the dungeon itself goes under oath: **descents
as theorems.**

```
Build a Dungeon Master tribute at the level of a modern commercial
grid crawler. Single-file browser game, zero assets. Original floors
and creatures — the real Dungeon Master (FTL Games, 1987) is
copyrighted. Four champions in one first-person body, skills that
advance only by use, rune-sequenced magic on the canon power scale
LO through MON, and a survival economy of food, water, and torchlight
that kills the idle on its own.

One owner writes the whole game. Then harsh sub-agent critics: the
three visual lenses plus a DUNGEON-MASTER-FIDELITY critic judging
mechanics against the 1987 canon.

The harness requirement is absolute: the solution is authored
objectives executed by a routing bot with a survival policy, proven
to WIN at speedrun pace AND at human pace; a null party must LOSE to
the dungeon's own arithmetic; each survival system ablated must show
its weight (starvation, dehydration, runelessness, darkness); and
every dungeon law — hunger, torchlight, keys, ZO, pits, plates,
teleporters, XP-by-use — gets an isolated machine-checked proof.
Grade against the commercial bar, call out fixes that didn't land in
the pixels, report the honest number.
```
